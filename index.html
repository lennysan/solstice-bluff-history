Takeaways (visual read)

Not a survey — a plain-language look at these frames only.

Not obviously alarming at this zoom: the bluff does not look like it has raced inland toward the house between 2004 and 2024.

  • 1993 is before the house (listing year built: 2004). Useful for the natural shoreline shape, not for setback to a building.
  • From 2004 through 2024, Bluff Trail still runs between the 233 Solstice cluster and the rocky drop to the ocean. The cliff edge stays on the ocean side of that trail in every color frame.
  • There is no clear “the cliff ate the house lots” jump at Google Earth resolution. Vegetation and lawn between the roofs and the edge change year to year (trees grow or thin; grass color shifts), which can fool the eye more than a true edge move.
  • The homes sit close to an active ocean bluff — normal Sea Ranch risk — but these images alone do not show rapid multi-decade recession into the building footprint.
  • Small retreat (a few feet per decade) would be hard to see here. The inspector’s stills-with-scale method is the right way to estimate feet.

This is a desk read of satellite/aerial tiles, not geologic or survey advice. Treat the inspector’s measured retreat (and any local geology report) as the authority if they differ from this impression.
